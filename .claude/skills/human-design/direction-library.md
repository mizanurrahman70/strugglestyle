# Direction Library

Each direction is a real design tradition with its own logic. Pick three that are **far apart** for each client.
Do not mix all of them into one "modern" soup. Commit to the logic of the chosen tradition.

Font names are suggestions from Google Fonts. Rotate them between clients; never default to the first option.

---

## 1. Editorial / Magazine
**Feels like:** a well-designed print magazine or weekend newspaper supplement.
**Good for:** fashion, hospitality, food, culture, consultancies with a story, premium manufacturing.
- **Type:** high-contrast serif display (Fraunces, Newsreader, Playfair Display *only at large optical sizes*,
  Instrument Serif, DM Serif Display) + a plain grotesk or a book serif for body (Source Serif 4, Libre Franklin,
  Public Sans, IBM Plex Sans). Use italics as a voice, not a decoration.
- **Layout:** asymmetric 12-col grid, big headlines that break columns, pull quotes, drop caps, captions under every
  image, running section numbers ("03 / Process"), bylines and dates.
- **Color:** mostly paper (warm off-white #F4F0E8-ish) and ink (#141414-ish) + one editorial accent.
- **Details:** hairline rules, figure numbers, photo credits, footnotes.
- **Avoid:** cards with shadows; everything sits on the page like print.

## 2. Swiss / International Typographic
**Feels like:** a 1960s Zurich poster, or a modern design-agency site.
**Good for:** architecture, engineering, tech B2B, logistics, data-heavy services.
- **Type:** one strong grotesk in 2–3 weights (Archivo, Schibsted Grotesk, Hanken Grotesk, Geologica, Familjen Grotesk).
  Big size contrast (e.g. 14px body vs 120px display).
- **Layout:** strict visible grid, flush-left ragged-right, lots of white space, content hung from a baseline,
  numbers and labels in small caps or mono.
- **Color:** black/white + one pure signal color (red, cobalt, signal orange).
- **Details:** grid lines shown as structure, index numbers, tabular figures for data.
- **Avoid:** rounded corners, gradients, soft shadows.

## 3. Craft / Workshop / Material
**Feels like:** a maker's workbook, a label printed on kraft paper, a factory notebook.
**Good for:** manufacturing, textiles, bakeries, coffee, furniture, ceramics, agriculture.
- **Type:** a sturdy slab or wood-type-inspired face (Zilla Slab, Bitter, Alfa Slab One for display; avoid Roboto Slab),
  or a characterful serif, + a mono or typewriter face for labels (JetBrains Mono, IBM Plex Mono, Courier Prime).
- **Layout:** tags, stamps, spec tables, batch numbers, measurement marks, stitched/dashed borders.
- **Color:** taken from the actual materials (cotton, indigo, kraft, clay, steel, wood).
- **Details:** real texture photos, hand-drawn annotations (SVG), "spec sheet" style product info.
- **Avoid:** fake grunge overlays; texture must come from real photos or precise linework.

## 4. Neo-Brutalist / Raw
**Feels like:** an independent studio, a zine, an indie record label.
**Good for:** creative agencies, streetwear, events, startups that want to look bold, youth brands.
- **Type:** heavy grotesk or condensed (Anton, Bricolage Grotesque, Big Shoulders Display, Darker Grotesque) + mono.
- **Layout:** visible borders (2px solid), blocks that touch, oversized type, deliberate collisions, marquee text.
- **Color:** flat, loud, limited (e.g. off-white + black + acid yellow).
- **Details:** hard offset shadows (4px 4px 0 #000), raw HTML-like lists, cursor effects used sparingly.
- **Avoid:** being unreadable; hierarchy still has to work.

## 5. Quiet Luxury / Catalogue
**Feels like:** a high-end product catalogue, a boutique hotel, a jeweller.
**Good for:** premium fashion, real estate, interiors, beauty, high-end hospitality.
- **Type:** refined serif or elegant sans with wide tracking for labels (Cormorant *at display sizes only*,
  Gilda Display, Italiana, Bodoni Moda, Marcellus) + a light body sans (Jost, Manrope at 300–400).
- **Layout:** very generous space, few elements per screen, large full-bleed photography, small centered labels.
- **Color:** muted, tonal (stone, sand, deep green, oxblood); almost no saturated color.
- **Details:** thin rules, small caps, slow cross-fade transitions, numbered collections.
- **Avoid:** gold gradients, script fonts, too many CTAs.

## 6. Warm Humanist / Friendly
**Feels like:** a local business people trust: clinic, school, NGO, family restaurant.
**Good for:** healthcare, education, nonprofits, local services, family businesses.
- **Type:** humanist sans with warmth (Nunito Sans *carefully*, Figtree, Atkinson Hyperlegible, Lexend, Rubik)
  + a soft serif for headings (Lora, Literata, Young Serif).
- **Layout:** relaxed, readable, real photos of real people, clear steps, honest prices.
- **Color:** warm and slightly desaturated (terracotta, ochre, teal, cream), never "SaaS blue".
- **Details:** hand-drawn underlines or circles (SVG), photo captions with names, simple illustrated icons.
- **Avoid:** childish blob illustrations, stock "diverse team high-five" photos.

## 7. Technical / Industrial Documentation
**Feels like:** an engineering manual, a datasheet, an aircraft panel.
**Good for:** industrial B2B, energy, hardware, software tools, labs.
- **Type:** mono + condensed grotesk (IBM Plex Mono, Martian Mono; Barlow Condensed, Saira Condensed). Avoid Space Mono, it is overused.
- **Layout:** spec tables, diagrams, callout leaders, part numbers, revision stamps, coordinate labels.
- **Color:** dark or blueprint-like, with one warning/signal color.
- **Details:** SVG technical drawings, unit labels, status indicators.
- **Avoid:** fake terminals and "hacker" green text unless the client is literally a dev tool.

## 8. Regional / Cultural Modern
**Feels like:** modern design rooted in a specific place's visual culture.
**Good for:** clients whose origin matters (Bangladeshi textiles, Middle Eastern hospitality, Japanese food, etc.).
- **Type:** pair a Latin face with a high-quality local script (e.g. Hind Siliguri / Noto Serif Bengali / Baloo Da 2
  for Bangla; Noto Kufi / IBM Plex Sans Arabic for Arabic). Design both scripts with equal care.
- **Layout:** borrow structure from local artefacts: rickshaw art, nakshi kantha stitch patterns, shop signage,
  textile borders, carved screens. Use them as **structure** (dividers, grids, borders), not clip-art.
- **Color:** from real local materials and places, researched, not stereotyped.
- **Avoid:** cliché ornaments pasted on a generic template.

---

## How to pick three
1. One **safe** direction that the client will feel comfortable with (closest to their stated taste).
2. One **strong** direction that is clearly the most distinctive (what you would choose as their designer).
3. One **surprise** direction from a tradition they did not mention, but with a clear reason it fits.
